# Using AI: How It’s Changing the Way I Think

Over the past few months, I’ve been using AI for a variety of tasks — from writing code and documentation to brainstorming and drafting blog posts like this one.

Sometimes, I find myself overestimating AI’s capabilities — expecting it to handle complex tasks with ease. Other times, I underestimate it — thinking it won’t manage even the simplest steps. In reality, its capabilities are evolving daily. But in our rush to judge how powerful or limited AI is, we’ve mostly overlooked something important: **its impact on our own thinking**.

In most discussions, the impact of AI on humans is framed negatively — how it may reduce our need to think or how it makes us lazy. While I’ve had those thoughts too, I’ve also noticed something quite the opposite: AI, when used properly, has helped me **think more critically**.

---

## Teaching AI Is Teaching Yourself

One of the first things I realized is that to get meaningful answers from AI, I need to *teach* it first.

Whether it’s by providing a knowledge graph, background context, or even just a well-thought-out prompt, AI needs to be coached. Think of it like onboarding a new teammate — the clearer your guidance, the better the outcome. If I explain the full problem clearly and in steps, the responses are far more accurate.

And that process — of breaking down a problem, structuring it, and feeding it into a system — forces **me** to think more clearly too.

---

## Capability Without Context Is Just Potential

It’s easy to treat AI as this all-powerful engine. But AI on its own is like a person with raw intelligence but no training. What makes one person or organization better than another? Often, it comes down to exposure — better data, better feedback, better examples.

AI is no different. Its intelligence is a **capability**, not a superpower. It needs the right input. That’s where we come in. The quality and depth of information we give AI define the quality of the output we get back.

---

## Feynman Was Right — Again

Richard Feynman once said: *“If you want to truly understand something, try explaining it to someone else.”*

That’s exactly what prompting AI does. When I try to write a good prompt, I’m forced to slow down and question my assumptions:

- What am I actually trying to do?
- What context is necessary for AI to help me?
- What would a beginner need to know to follow along?

By clearly articulating a problem, I not only get better responses from AI — I also get better at understanding the problem myself.

Even a simple prompt like:

> “Let’s go step by step and apply one change at a time…”

…adds structure to how I approach the problem.

---

## We’ve Seen This Before — Coding Interviews

This phenomenon isn’t new. Take the evolution of coding interviews.

A decade ago, interviews often focused on syntax and basic language features. Over time, as answers became easily searchable online, the focus shifted to data structures, algorithms, and deeper problem-solving — things that couldn’t be easily Googled.

Today, algorithmic questions are standard, not elite. It’s debatable whether this has made us smarter — but it has certainly **raised the baseline**.

We adapted.

And AI may trigger a similar shift — one where superficial understanding gets replaced by deeper reasoning, because that’s what gives us the edge when working with AI tools.

---

## So… Will AI Make Us Smarter?

Honestly, I don’t know. I don’t have a crystal ball. And I’m definitely not qualified to define what “smart” even means.

But here’s what I *can* say, from experience:

To use AI well, I often have to pause, slow down, and think things through step by step. That’s the exact opposite of lazy thinking. Once I do that, AI can take care of the repetitive tasks. And I get to spend more time thinking critically — analyzing results, designing better systems, and making better decisions.

Whether this trend continues or shifts again is yet to be seen.

But for now, AI isn’t just a tool for automation — **it’s a tool that reflects and refines our thinking.**
